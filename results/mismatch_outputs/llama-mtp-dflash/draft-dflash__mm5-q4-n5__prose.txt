The fog rolled in thick and gray, swallowing the horizon until the world was nothing but the rhythmic *thump-thump* of the waves against the rocks. Elias, the lighthouse keeper, climbed the spiral stairs with the ease of a man who had done it ten thousand times. He adjusted the Fresnel lens, checking the oil levels, his routine as ingrained as his heartbeat.

But tonight, the rhythm was wrong.

For decades, the light had swept in a steady, predictable arc: three seconds on, three seconds off. A beacon of safety, not a message. Yet, as Elias watched the beam cut through the mist, he noticed a stutter. A pause. A quick double-flash. Then silence.

*Dot-dot-dot. Dash-dash-dash. Dot-dot-dot.*
